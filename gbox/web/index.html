    Fans and temperaturefrom the gbox service log, same time scale as above
    
    
    fan0fan1chip temperatureboard sensor (what the fan controller and the stock UI use)
    
    
    
    
    
    
    The hottest chip is the one to watch: it is what the warmest silicon sees, and the firmware's own chip-temperature field is the board's average, not its hottest (on the unit this was built against the hottest chip ran a steady 11 °C above that average). The red band runs from the sustained level up to the peak reading in each bucket; the dashed line is the serious threshold. The board sensor reads lower and is shown because the fan controller steers on it. Hover a marker for the event text; a gap after an S means the service was down, not the miner. When served, the third panel counts board resets per 5 minutes, and the shaded band marks a bucket worth a look, the same marks the three-day chart uses. 
    
    
    Board reset: the miner's own doing. Its firmware reinitializes the hashboard when a chip stops answering; gbox only counts what the firmware reports. Never commanded by anyone, so it has no marker: it is drawn as bars. Soft restart: an action. The controller rebooted through its API, by the watchdog when shares freeze or the miner goes unreachable (the W marker), or by you from Controls (▼). Power cycle: an action. The plug cutting power, by the watchdog when two soft restarts have failed, or by you (P). A reset is what the miner does to itself when a chip misbehaves; a restart is what gbox does to the miner when it stops working; a cycle is what gbox does when a restart cannot reach it. That order is the ladder.

TP-Link's Kasa plug listing: kasasmart.com/us/products/smart-plugs (the three marked "Slim with Energy Monitoring" are the metering ones). How to set one up: docs/power-cycle.md.

    chipgoodbadbad %bad/mingood, relative
    
      
    A chip is flagged weak below 70 percent of the best chip's good nonces or above 40 bad nonces, and failing below 30 percent or above 30 percent bad. A failing chip at the factory clock can reset the whole board; lowering the clock with gbox plan usually stops it.

    Clock
    
        
    
        
    25 MHz steps, applied live through the manual power plan, no reboot. Asks for the password.
    Sets the chip clock through the manual power plan, live, with no reboot. Lower it 25 MHz at a time when the board resets or a chip's bad share climbs; raise it when a run stays clean for hours. The Clock trials table below compares every clock you have run. Asks for the miner password because it changes what the miner does.
    
      
    Fan target
    
        
    
        
    Board-sensor temperature the fans hold. 65 is the coolest this firmware allows. The fans spike for a few minutes after any settings change.
    The board-sensor temperature the fan controller holds: the fans speed up until the board reads this. 65 is the coolest this firmware allows. Lower keeps the chips cooler at the cost of noise. The fans spike for a few minutes after any settings change; that is the fan daemon restarting, not a fault. No password.
    
      
    Power plan preset
    
        
    
        
    —
    Puts the miner back on one of the firmware's own power plans and clears the manual clock. On this unit the factory preset is the clock that caused the reset loop, so use it knowingly. Asks for the password.
    
      
    Controller
    
        
    
        
    Reboots the controller; hashing resumes after 60-90 s. Asks for the password. The watchdog is told, so it does not restart again on top.
    Reboots the controller, the small computer that runs the miner. The hashboard reinitializes and hashing resumes after 60 to 90 seconds; the manual clock survives. Use it when the miner answers but has stopped producing shares, or came up without its board. The watchdog is told, so it does not restart on top. Asks for the password.
    
      
    Power and holds (through the gbox service)
    
        
    
        
    
        
    —
    Hold tells the service the miner will be unreachable on purpose, so the watchdog does not read the outage as a freeze and start restarting and power-cycling. Use it before you pull the cord, swap a power supply, or move the unit. It ends by itself as soon as the miner is hashing again (two samples in a row, about a minute), or when the time runs out. Release ends a hold early if you changed your mind. Neither needs a password: a hold only stops the software judging.
    Off opens the smart plug's relay and holds the watchdog with no expiry: the miner stays off until you press On. Use it for the night, a hot afternoon, or work on the unit. It asks for the miner password, because it stops hashing. Cycle is Off, a pause, then On, for a miner that is misbehaving but still answering. After On the watchdog waits the settle gap before judging anything, so a boot is not mistaken for a freeze.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Clock trialsone row per clock and fan target the miner has run, from the service log, newest first
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    Hold each clock for hours, not minutes, then compare rows. Bad share is the worst chip's bad nonces as a share of its nonces in that run; compare shares, not counts, because a slower clock attempts fewer. ≥ means the chip dropped off the weak list part-way, which is an improvement. Board resets over zero end a trial. Accepted/hr is what the pool pays on but follows its share difficulty as much as the miner: use hashrate for throughput. HW error with ~ is the firmware's running average, from samples logged before the nonce totals were kept. A row ends when the clock or fan target changes, the miner restarts, or the log has a gap over five minutes; runs under 20 minutes are hidden. Method: docs/clock-tuning.md.

    whenwhowhatand then
    
    
    Everything that changed the miner, from the service event log: soft restarts and power cycles by the watchdog and the plug, your own clock, fan and restart actions (you), and each service start. "Miner back after" is measured from the event to the first good sample after the outage it caused. The raw log is in the Service section below.

    Termsthe words this page uses, in the order you meet them
    
    
    bad share
    bad nonces over all nonces in a window, in percent. The number that means the same thing at every clock; a faster clock attempts more nonces, so a bad count does not.
    board reset
    the miner reinitializing its own hashboard when a chip stops answering; counted from the firmware, never commanded, drawn as bars rather than markers. A burst of resets with a flat bad share is the chain going silent, not bad nonces.
    soft restart
    the controller rebooted through its API: by the watchdog when shares freeze or the miner goes unreachable (the W marker), or by you from Controls (▼). Hashing resumes in 60 to 90 s; the manual clock survives.
    power cycle
    the plug cutting power and restoring it: by the watchdog when two soft restarts have failed (the P marker), or by you. The only thing that clears a freeze.
    worst chip
    the chip with the highest bad share in the window, named board.chip.
    hottest chip
    the warmest of the board's chips, from the cgminer log, the only place the firmware reports it (its API's chip-temperature field is the average). The service reads the log every five minutes.
    level
    the sustained hottest-chip temperature: the median of the log's 5-second readings over one read. The number the tile, the badge and the thresholds use.
    peak
    the highest single 5-second reading in the same window. Information, not the flag: a peak 8 to 10 °C over the level a few times an hour is normal operation, and a flag on it would never go out.
    cold start
    the first half hour after a power-on, when a marginal chip fails first. Compare cold starts, not only steady state.
    hold
    the watchdog standing down because you said the miner would be unreachable on purpose. Lifts when hashing resumes.
    settle gap
    the minutes after a restart or a power-on in which the watchdog judges nothing, so a boot is not mistaken for a freeze.
    ladder
    what the watchdog does to a hung miner, in order: a soft restart, a second one, then the plug, under daily caps.
    freeze
    the controller dropping off the network with the board idle. A soft restart cannot reach it; only a power cycle clears it.
