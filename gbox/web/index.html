    Fans and temperaturefrom the gbox service log, same time scale as above
    
    
    fan0fan1chip temperatureboard sensor (what the fan controller and the stock UI use)
    
    
    
    
    Chip temperature is the one to watch: it is what the silicon sees. The board sensor reads lower and is shown because the fan controller steers on it. Markers: ▼ a dashboard button, W the watchdog, P the plug, S a service start (a gap after an S means the service was down, not the miner); hover one for the event text. 
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Errors over three daysbad share of all chips and of the worst chip per half hour, the clock, and board resets, from the service log
    
    
    
    
    Bad share is bad nonces over all nonces in each half hour, summed from the log's counters across boots, so it means the same thing at every clock. Resets are the board's reinitializations in that half hour: a burst with a flat share is the chain going silent, not bad nonces. The worst chip is named board.chip; before 0.6.0 the log only named chips the firmware flagged, so older half hours may show none. Hover for the counts.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Power at the wallfrom the gbox service log, same time scale as above
    
    
    
    
    
    
    The reading comes from a TP-Link Kasa smart plug on its original local protocol; no cloud account, nothing leaves the LAN. Kasa plugs this tool can read a meter from: KP115, KP125 and the end-of-life HS110. Metering plugs whose newer firmware needs the TP-Link account (KLAP), not driven yet: KP125M, EP25; the HS300 strip meters per outlet and is not supported. Plugs that only switch, still fine for power-cycling but with no meter: HS100 (end of life), HS103, HS105, KP100, KP105, KP401, EP10, the outdoor EP40, EP40A and EP40M, and the strips HS107, KP200, KP303, KP400. TP-Link's Kasa plug listing: kasasmart.com/us/products/smart-plugs (the three marked "Slim with Energy Monitoring" are the metering ones). How to set one up: docs/power-cycle.md.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    Chipsgood and bad nonces since the miner booted; bad/min since this page opened
    
      
    chipgoodbadbad %bad/mingood, relative
    
      
    A chip is flagged weak below 70 percent of the best chip's good nonces or above 40 bad nonces, and failing below 30 percent or above 30 percent bad. A failing chip at the factory clock can reset the whole board; lowering the clock with gbox plan usually stops it.

    Clock trialsone row per clock and fan target the miner has run, from the service log, newest first
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    Hold each clock for hours, not minutes, then compare rows. Bad share is the worst chip's bad nonces as a share of its nonces in that run; compare shares, not counts, because a slower clock attempts fewer. ≥ means the chip dropped off the weak list part-way, which is an improvement. Board resets over zero end a trial. Accepted/hr is what the pool pays on but follows its share difficulty as much as the miner: use hashrate for throughput. HW error with ~ is the firmware's running average, from samples logged before the nonce totals were kept. A row ends when the clock or fan target changes, the miner restarts, or the log has a gap over five minutes; runs under 20 minutes are hidden. Method: docs/clock-tuning.md.
